🛫 Aerial Training Modules Included

Aerial play is usually the first thing that separates one rank from the next, and it is also the hardest thing to practise without structure. The toolkit breaks it into short, repeatable modules:

  1. Lift-off basics: consistent first jump, second jump timing and boost management in a straight vertical climb.
  2. Angle control: hitting the ball from a tilted approach instead of a flat, predictable line.
  3. Air roll fundamentals: directional air roll in small doses so your car ends up where you intended.
  4. Fast aerials: the boost-and-jump sequence drilled until it happens without thinking.
  5. Wall to air: reading the wall bounce and converting it into a controlled aerial touch.
  6. Recovery aerials: landing on your wheels after an awkward attempt instead of leaving your teammate alone.
  7. Redirects: changing the ball's direction mid-flight with a deliberate touch rather than a hopeful one.

Each module includes a short description, a recommended repetition count and a place to mark how the session felt. You do not need to finish a module in one sitting; the toolkit remembers where you left off.

Open the training hub, pick a module and follow the repetition targets. Each time you finish a set, mark how it went. That single habit is what makes the analytics section useful later.

After a match, open the replay notes tab and add one or two lines about what went wrong or right. Tag it as a rotation issue, a mechanical miss or a decision error. Over a couple of weeks these notes become the most useful page in the whole toolkit.

📊 Reading Your Competitive Analytics

The analytics panel is deliberately simple. It does not overwhelm you with numbers, it shows you three things: how much you practised, what you practised, and which mistakes keep appearing in your notes.

  • Practice volume: total reps and total sessions across the week, so you can spot the weeks where training quietly stopped.
  • Focus balance: the share of your time spent on aerials versus car control versus kickoffs, which is often very different from what you thought you were doing.
  • Mistake tags: the most common tags in your replay notes, which point directly at the next thing worth drilling.
  • Streaks: a simple count of consecutive days with at least one logged session.

Use it once a week. Ten minutes of reading your own trends is usually worth more than another hour of unfocused free play.
