Welcome.

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Economics at Virginia Tech. My research is in microeconomic theory, particularly networks and game theory.

I study how people make decisions when they know their own connections but have limited information about the broader network. My work examines how their beliefs shape behavior, what they learn through repeated interactions, and how limited information affects policy interventions.

In my job market paper, I study whom a policymaker should remove from a network when only the removed individual’s neighbors observe the intervention. The impact depends on both the person’s connections and what others know about the removal.

My teaching emphasizes active learning and economic reasoning. I co-developed Marginal Falls Economic Quests, a collection of interactive games for learning economics through decision-making.

I am on the 2026–2027 academic job market.
